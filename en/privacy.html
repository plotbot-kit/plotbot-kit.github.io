No cookies

This site sets no cookies and shows no ads. Fonts, images and scripts come from the same server as the page. Your browser contacts servers other than this site's in three cases: when GoatCounter counts a visit, when you click to load the video from YouTube, and when you submit the notification form. The sections below describe each one.

The site stores two things in your browser, and each only when you click: your language choice and, if you object to visitor counting, that objection (more on this below). About the language choice: when you click a language link (such as "Deutsch" or "English"), your browser remembers that choice in its local storage (localStorage, key lang), so the start page opens in your language next time. This entry never leaves your browser, and you can delete it at any time in your browser settings. Under Section 25(2) no. 2 of the German Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act (TDDDG), this needs no consent, because the entry only stores the language you chose.

Visitor counting with GoatCounter

We count how often each page is viewed. We use GoatCounter for this, a service run by Martin Tournoij, Ireland. GoatCounter says the data is stored on servers at Hetzner Online GmbH in Germany and Finland, both in the EU.

When you open a page, your browser sends a request to plotbot.goatcounter.com. It contains the address of the page, including any parameters after the question mark, its title, the page you came from (the referrer) and the width of your screen. As with any request on the web, the server also sees your IP address, your browser's language setting and its user agent (a line of text naming your browser and operating system). GoatCounter works out your country from the IP address. It counts how often each page is viewed. Separately, it counts where views came from and which browser, operating system, country, language and screen width they came with.

GoatCounter sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser when it counts. According to GoatCounter, it doesn't write your IP address or the full user agent to its database; it keeps both in memory for up to eight hours and then discards them. That's how it can tell that several views belong to one visit. In the statistics we see totals, not individual visitors.

The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR: we have a legitimate interest in knowing which pages people read, so we can improve the site. You can object to being counted: switch off counting in this browser. The link opens the start page. Your browser then notes in its local storage (key skipgc) that it shouldn't be counted, and confirms this with a message. The same link switches counting back on, and so does reloading that address. The link needs JavaScript. With JavaScript switched off, an invisible image counts the view instead; you can prevent that by blocking plotbot.goatcounter.com in your browser.

For more, see GoatCounter's privacy policy.

Google says that in this mode it doesn't use the view to personalise YouTube or ads for you. What else Google processes, and whether the player sets cookies or stores anything else in your browser, is up to Google; we have no control over it.

The legal basis is your consent under Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and Section 25(1) TDDDG, which you give by clicking the picture. You can withdraw your consent at any time for the future; reloading or closing the page is enough. The site doesn't remember your consent: reload the page and the player is gone, and your browser won't contact YouTube again until you click once more.

Google may transfer data to Google LLC in the USA. Google LLC states that it has certified its adherence to the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework; in its decision of 10 July 2023, the European Commission found that companies taking part in the framework provide an adequate level of data protection. For details, see Google's privacy policy.

With JavaScript switched off, the picture is an ordinary link to the video on YouTube, where Google's privacy policy applies.

Flashing the firmware with ESP Launchpad

The “Flash the firmware” button on the Flash the firmware page is a link to ESP Launchpad at espressif.github.io. Until you click it, your browser doesn't contact that site. Launchpad is a web page run by Espressif Systems, the company that makes the ESP32 chip. Like our site, it's hosted on GitHub Pages; when you open it, GitHub processes the technical data described under “Hosting on GitHub Pages” above. Espressif is responsible for Launchpad, not us.

Launchpad downloads a configuration file and the firmware from our website – so again from GitHub Pages – and sends the firmware over the USB cable to the board. On 13 November 2026 we checked what happens when Launchpad opens with our configuration file: the browser loaded Launchpad from espressif.github.io and, apart from that, only our configuration file and our logo, and Launchpad set no cookies. We couldn't observe the flashing itself in that check. Espressif can change its page without telling us.
